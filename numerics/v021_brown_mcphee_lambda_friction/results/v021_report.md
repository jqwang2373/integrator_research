Generated by `run_v021.py`.

## Purpose

- Replace v019's Gaussian/tanh Stribeck-inspired load with a Brown-McPhee-style continuous velocity friction curve.
- Keep the same quaternion endpoint DAE and multiplier-dependent normal load so the residual still differentiates through friction and Lagrange multipliers.
- Compare Gauss4, Gauss6, and embedded adaptive Gauss64 on smooth and sharp Brown-McPhee velocity scales.
- JAX kernels are warmed before timed fixed/adaptive runs.

## Interpretation

- This is closer to Brown-McPhee than v019 because the friction curve uses the continuous `tanh(4v/vs)` Coulomb term plus the rational stiction/Stribeck bump and a viscous term.
- The model is applied componentwise to the pivot angular velocity with friction magnitude scaled by the stage reaction-load norm, so it is still a fixed-pivot surrogate rather than a full revolute-joint friction implementation.
- Smooth-case Gauss6 order estimates above six should be read as finite-grid/pre-asymptotic evidence of very small errors, not as a claim that the method exceeds its nominal sixth order.
- A smooth Brown-McPhee velocity scale tests whether Gauss6 keeps the high-order advantage under a more realistic continuous friction curve.
- A sharp Brown-McPhee velocity scale tests whether v020's adaptive advantage persists when the low-speed stiction bump is steeper.
- The method is still smooth velocity-based friction, not a stick-slip complementarity solver.
