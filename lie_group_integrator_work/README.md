Current experimental read:
- v002 shows fourth-order Lie-group kinematics is easy to get with CF4/RKMK4, but invariant preservation is a separate issue.
- v003 shows the SBEL rA dynamics baseline is reproducible with modern package compatibility patches, and gives roughly first-order velocity/acceleration convergence for the tested driven mechanisms.
- v004 shows `lie_midpoint_yoshida4` is near fourth order on the Euler top while preserving energy and body angular-momentum norm to Newton tolerance; its negative substep is the main caveat for friction/contact DAEs.
- v005 improves on v004 for smooth conservative rigid-body dynamics: `gauss_lie4` measured orientation order 3.987, omega order 3.989, and 100s h=0.05 spatial momentum drift 5.854e-07 while preserving energy/body momentum norm to near machine precision. It costs about 8.8x explicit RKMK4 in the Python long-run prototype.
- v006 shows `gauss_lie4` remains fourth order on a constrained fixed-pivot rigid body: orientation order 3.986, omega order 3.987, exact reconstructed position/velocity constraints, and 100s h=0.05 energy drift 1.495e-07 versus 2.732e-04 for explicit RKMK4.
- v007 shows the same idea works in an explicit-multiplier absolute-coordinate stage solve only when the stage residual includes position, velocity, and acceleration consistency. The best v007 variant, `absolute_gauss_lie4_acc_projected`, measured orientation order 3.970, omega order 3.976, zero endpoint position/velocity constraint error after projection, and 20s h=0.05 energy drift 1.495e-07.
- v008 removes the post-step projection by adding endpoint `u/r/v/omega` unknowns and endpoint position/velocity constraints inside Newton. It preserves the v007 best accuracy: orientation order 3.970, omega order 3.976, endpoint constraint about 1e-16 at h=0.05, and 20s h=0.05 energy drift 1.495e-07. Cost increases because the finite-difference Newton system grows from 42 to 54 variables.
- v009 adds friction torque `tau_f(omega) = -c_v omega - mu tanh(omega / eps)` to the reduced reference ODE and the absolute-coordinate rotational DAE residual. With smooth regularization (`eps=0.50`), `absolute_gauss_lie4_endpoint` measures orientation order 3.979 and omega order 4.009. With sharper Coulomb-like regularization (`eps=0.05`), observed endpoint orientation order drops to 2.462 and omega order to 2.064. In 5s h=0.05 runs, both friction cases have negative final energy change, zero measured positive step energy increase, negative friction power, and endpoint constraints around 1e-14 or better.
- v010 validates the paper's AD/Jacobian-engineering motivation on the local endpoint DAE prototype. A JAX `jacfwd` Jacobian gives roundoff-level agreement with the finite-difference Newton solve. Warm h=0.05 order-run speedup is 33.40x for smooth friction and 38.59x for sharper friction; 5s dissipation-run speedup is 35.42x and 29.50x. This does not change the integrator order, but removes the dominant practical bottleneck for larger friction/contact tests.
- v011 validates the paper's quaternion derivative-transport identity directly. With scalar-first quaternions and `p(theta)=exp(theta/2) * p0`, analytic `T_exp` matches direct AD of the quaternion update with max absolute error 1.110e-16. For a nonlinear friction-like load depending on position, quaternion, local rotation, velocity, angular velocity, and multiplier-like normal load, transported `Pi(g)` and `Theta(g)` match direct composite AD with max absolute error 2.429e-15. Central finite differences only agree to about 2.165e-10, showing why the AD transport route is preferable.
- v012 moves the endpoint DAE residual itself to scalar-first unit quaternions with right-action update `p(theta)=p0 * exp(theta/2)`, matching the earlier `R exp(theta)` convention. The right-action `T_exp` check matches AD to 1.110e-16. The quaternion endpoint method preserves v010's behavior: smooth friction orientation order 3.979 and omega order 4.009; sharper friction orientation order 2.462 and omega order 2.064. Against v010 SO(3), 5s h=0.05 final orientation differences are 1.771e-15 for smooth friction and 2.996e-15 for sharper friction, with unit-quaternion error at 1e-16 scale.
- v013 performs the first method-level higher-order comparison on the verified quaternion residual. The three-stage Gauss6 endpoint method reaches smooth-friction orientation order 5.994 and omega order 6.226.
