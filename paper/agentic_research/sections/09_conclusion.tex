\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A coding agent carried a numerical-analysis research program from a prototype to a manuscript with
a new theorem, a machine-checked proof and a defensible numerical study. What made the outcome
trustworthy was not the agent's judgment but artefacts that turned judgments into checks: versioned
experiments that could not be edited away, a ledger that tied claims to records, a proof assistant
that would not accept a gap, memory across sessions, boundaries on what could be run without asking,
and a manuscript whose numbers are read from files. The few residual judgments were the human's (whether
the paper was good, whether a figure looked right, which model to keep), and the practical question
for agentic research is then not how to remove the human but how to arrange the work so that these
judgments land on the right pages.
